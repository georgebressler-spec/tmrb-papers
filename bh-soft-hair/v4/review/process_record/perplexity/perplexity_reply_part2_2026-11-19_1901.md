ReviewAgents also needs careful interpretation: its main evaluation rewards resemblance to
human reviews, including lexical/semantic and sentiment similarity, and uses GPT-4o for its
pairwise Review Arena. That is evidence about generated-review alignment, not an
independent demonstration of improved scientific error detection or better human final
decisions.
For your specific evidence-type rule—weighting agreement differently when it comes from
reasoning, retrieval or execution—I found no directly matching empirical evaluation in the
checked sources. Family/model provenance weighting is not the same thing as weighting these
three evidential categories. The dependence-aware source above tests the former, not the latter.
None found.
I found no published evaluation combining all of the following: independently produced multi-
LLM reviews, prospectively registered criteria, reasoning/retrieval/execution-based evidence
weighting rather than vote counting, family-dependence adjustment, human final decisions,
and after-action reviews that update the next round’s procedure.
The closest identified studies evaluate subsets: multi-agent review generation, feedback
compared with human reviews, or dependence-aware answer aggregation. None of those
establishes the effectiveness of your complete workflow.
A defensible description is therefore: “a proposed review procedure informed by component
evidence and analogous practices,” not “a validated review method.” This distinction also
preserves the counter-evidence: human–AI combinations do not automatically outperform the
better standalone participant, and dependence discounting does not automatically improve
accuracy.
(g) Least certain: Ladha’s full theorem conditions, uninspected experimental details and
supplements, separately confirmed proceedings metadata/acceptance, and the completeness of
the negative search for an exact combined-method evaluation.
